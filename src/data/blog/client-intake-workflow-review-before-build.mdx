A workflow prototype is useful when it helps a team make service decisions. It is not useful when it only proves that the colors are nice.

## The prototype should behave like a real handoff

A serious prototype needs clear routing, short forms, ownership cues, status expectations, proof near decisions, mobile behavior, and a credible follow-up path. Those are the moments where clients decide whether the provider feels trustworthy.

## Mobile pressure reveals the truth

Desktop can hide weak hierarchy. Mobile cannot. Service choices, form fields, proof cues, contact actions, and status notes all need enough density to be useful without becoming cramped.

## Treat the review like a launch rehearsal

Ask specific questions during the review:

- Can a client find the right service path quickly?
- Does the page answer the first three ownership questions?
- Is the form clear about what happens next?
- Does the handoff feel shorter than it looks?
- Are trust signals near decisions instead of scattered randomly?

The best prototype gives stakeholders enough realism to approve a build plan with fewer surprises.
