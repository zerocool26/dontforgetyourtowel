An e-commerce demo is useful when it helps a team make product decisions. It is not useful when it only proves that the colors are nice.

## The demo should behave like a store

A serious prototype needs search, filtering, sorting, product detail, variants, reviews, cart logic, comparison, shipping cues, promo handling, and a credible checkout path. Those are the moments where buyers decide whether a store feels trustworthy.

## Mobile pressure reveals the truth

Desktop can hide weak hierarchy. Mobile cannot. Product cards, filter controls, sticky cart actions, trust cues, and checkout fields all need enough density to be useful without becoming cramped.

## Treat the review like a launch rehearsal

Ask specific questions during the demo:

- Can a buyer find the right product quickly?
- Does the product card answer the first three buying questions?
- Is the cart clear about price, delivery, and savings?
- Does checkout feel shorter than it looks?
- Are trust signals near decisions instead of scattered randomly?

The best demo gives stakeholders enough realism to approve a build plan with fewer surprises.
