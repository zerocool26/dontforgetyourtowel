# Olive Global Systems — Operational Calm Redesign Strategy

## 1. One-paragraph creative direction

Olive Global Systems should feel like operational calm inside real-world complexity: a mobile-first command surface where field constraints, trade execution, technology systems, and owner handoff are shown as one connected operating map. The design language is restrained, dark, tactile, and editorial, using olive-black depth, soft ivory text, one signature acid-olive accent, precise grid lines, quiet signal motion, and layered surfaces instead of a pile of glowing cards.

## A. Brand and positioning

### Core positioning sentence

Olive Global Systems coordinates trade work, facility service, managed technology, security, cloud, automation, ecommerce, AI, and business systems through one readiness-led delivery model.

### Audience

- Owners, operators, and small-to-mid-market leaders who need one accountable path through messy trade and technology problems.
- Facility managers and project stakeholders handling occupied buildings, repairs, modernization, uptime, and phased renovation pressure.
- Business owners who need field work and digital systems to connect without managing a scattered vendor bench.

### Emotional tone

Calm, capable, exact, grounded, modern, quietly premium, and operationally serious.

### Brand archetype

The Operator-Architect: part field command lead, part systems designer, part trusted technical partner.

### Differentiation

Olive Global Systems is not a generic contractor, MSP, agency, or service directory because it frames delivery around constraints and interfaces rather than isolated service labels. Contractors usually stop at trade execution, MSPs usually stop at infrastructure, agencies usually stop at campaigns or software, and directories only route leads. Olive Global Systems can present physical work, technology systems, operational support, and owner-ready handoff as a connected stack.

### Tagline directions

1. Built work. Managed systems. One operating view.
2. Where field execution meets systems intelligence.
3. Calm delivery for complex facilities, trades, and technology.

### Voice guide

Do:

- Say what the company does in the first screen.
- Lead with constraints, outcomes, and clarity.
- Use short, concrete language.
- Sound experienced, not flashy.
- Use technical language only when it improves precision.

Do not:

- Say “innovative solutions” without proof.
- Overuse “AI,” “next-gen,” “disruptive,” or “seamless.”
- Make every service sound equally urgent.
- Use generic SaaS claims.
- Let style hide what the company actually offers.

## B. Mobile-first information architecture

### Homepage narrative

1. Hero: instantly explain integrated trade + technology delivery.
2. Operating proof strip: field + cloud, lane-first clarity, readiness-led delivery.
3. Scope stack: show service lanes as connected layers, not disconnected cards.
4. Project readiness scanner: let visitors self-identify by pressure, not service category.
5. Premium proof: show cross-trade programs and owner outputs.
6. Delivery rhythm: define how Olive moves work from constraint to turnover.
7. Final CTA: invite a project brief.

### Ruthless prioritization

Keep on Home:

- The clearest positioning.
- One memorable operating-map visual.
- Service lanes at a glance.
- Need-based self-selection.
- Cross-trade proof.
- Simple CTA.

Move to Services:

- Full service menus.
- Managed technology detail.
- Pricing/scope nuance.
- Detailed service descriptions.

Move to About:

- Company story.
- Values.
- Operating philosophy.
- Team or credibility narrative.

Move to Contact HQ:

- Brief intake.
- Contact channels.
- Project context prompts.
- Conversion reassurance.

### Primary conversion path for first-time mobile visitors

Hero explanation → Find your lane → choose scanner path → read recommended starting lane → Send a project brief.

## D. Visual design system

### Color palette

- Background deep: `#030403`
- Background base: `#070807`
- Background lifted: `#0c100c`
- Surface: `rgba(15, 18, 14, 0.78)`
- Strong surface: `rgba(22, 27, 21, 0.92)`
- Primary text: `#f5f4ec`
- Soft text: `#d7ddcb`
- Muted text: `#a8b0a3`
- Border: `rgba(245, 244, 236, 0.12)`
- Strong border: `rgba(215, 247, 91, 0.28)`
- Signature accent: `#d7f75b`
- Secondary accent: `#12b5a6`

### Background system

Use layered radial gradients, subtle fixed grid lines, and very low-opacity noise. The background should feel dimensional without becoming a wallpaper.

### Accent logic

Use `#d7f75b` only for navigation signals, CTAs, small labels, rails, and key readiness indicators. Do not make every panel glow.

### Surface system

Use translucent dark panels with thin borders and one internal highlight. Prefer hard-edged command surfaces over rounded SaaS cards.

### Border system

Default border: `1px solid rgba(245, 244, 236, 0.12)`. Active/focus border: `rgba(215, 247, 91, 0.28)`.

### Shadow system

One primary shadow: `0 30px 80px -52px rgba(0, 0, 0, 0.9)`. Use shadow for depth, not decoration.

### Typography hierarchy

- Display: Space Grotesk, tight tracking, large editorial headlines.
- Body: Inter, 1.55–1.7 line height.
- Utility labels: JetBrains Mono, uppercase, high tracking.

### Spacing scale

- Mobile shell: `1rem` side margin.
- Section padding: `4.5rem` mobile, up to `7.5rem` desktop.
- Internal panel padding: `1rem–1.4rem` mobile, `2rem–3rem` desktop.

### Buttons

- Primary: signature accent, calm label, minimum 52px height.
- Secondary: bordered dark surface, no aggressive glow.
- Text links: clear affordance and strong focus outline.

### Link styles

Use quiet color transitions and underline/focus states. Avoid hidden links that only appear on hover.

### Card alternatives

Use stacks, rails, boards, strips, maps, panels, and timelines instead of generic cards.

### Dividers

Use thin horizontal rules and section background shifts, not decorative waves.

### Icons

Use monoline technical icons only where meaning is immediate. Prefer badges and labels over vague icons.

### Image treatment

When images are added later, crop editorially, desaturate slightly, and place inside command surfaces with captions. Avoid generic stock hero montages.

### Motion rules

Motion should clarify progress, active state, or hierarchy. Use slow ambient drift, scanner panel swaps, subtle tap feedback, and reveal-on-scroll. Respect reduced motion.

### Mobile nav behavior

Keep bottom mobile navigation if it helps thumb access, but avoid competing sticky elements. One sticky conversion affordance maximum.

### Sticky CTA behavior

Use only after the hero if enabled. Keep it small, safe-area aware, and not overlapping the bottom nav.

### Form styling

Use 16px minimum input font on mobile, 48px minimum field height, clear labels, visible focus, and helpful project-context prompts.

### Accessibility rules

- Maintain heading order.
- Use actual buttons for scanner tabs.
- Keep contrast high.
- Ensure 44px+ tap targets.
- Use `prefers-reduced-motion` fallbacks.
- Preserve keyboard focus states.

## E. Creative visual concepts

### 1. Field-to-Cloud Operating Map — chosen

Why it works: It directly visualizes the business difference: physical work and digital systems as one connected delivery layer.

Mobile appearance: A compact command surface with four stacked layers from site reality to owner output.

Interaction: Subtle animated signal lines and scanner anchors.

Affected sections: Hero, proof, service stack, delivery rhythm.

Technical implementation: CSS gradients, structured HTML, tiny JS only for scanner tabs.

Risk: Can become sci-fi. Avoid by using restrained color, labels, and real operating language.

### 2. Scope Stack

Why it works: It turns many services into a layered decision system instead of a directory.

Mobile appearance: Vertical stacked lanes with badges and a signal rail.

Interaction: Tap a lane to open its route.

Affected sections: Services and homepage service presentation.

Technical implementation: Astro map over service data, CSS variables for lane accents.

Risk: Too abstract. Avoid by keeping service names explicit.

### 3. Project Readiness Scanner

Why it works: It turns vague visitor intent into recommended starting points.

Mobile appearance: Four large tab buttons with one active recommendation panel.

Interaction: Button-driven panels with ARIA tab semantics.

Affected sections: Conversion path and Contact HQ intake.

Technical implementation: Small inline JS, progressive enhancement.

Risk: Can feel gimmicky. Avoid by using practical questions, not fake diagnostics.

### 4. Signal Cabinet

Why it works: It feels like a premium operations dashboard without becoming cyberpunk.

Mobile appearance: Narrow cabinet modules with status strips and owner outputs.

Interaction: Expand/collapse modules.

Affected sections: Services, proof, process.

Technical implementation: CSS grid, disclosure elements.

Risk: Too dashboard-heavy. Avoid excessive metrics and blinking states.

### 5. Readiness Rail

Why it works: It creates narrative continuity through the scroll.

Mobile appearance: A vertical rail connecting hero, scanner, services, proof, and CTA.

Interaction: Active section markers on scroll.

Affected sections: Entire homepage.

Technical implementation: CSS sticky rail plus optional IntersectionObserver.

Risk: Navigation clutter. Avoid if bottom nav already dominates.

## F. Motion and interaction system

- Scroll reveals: one entrance pattern only, already supported by `data-reveal`.
- Section transitions: use border, background shift, and spacing; no theatrical wipes.
- Hero animation: slow ambient glow drift and subtle signal line pulse.
- Touch interactions: translate 1–3px max; never bounce.
- Buttons: lift slightly and strengthen border/background.
- Cards/sections: hover/tap should reveal hierarchy, not hidden content needed for comprehension.
- Reduced motion: disable ambient drift, signal animation, transitions, and transform effects.
- Canvas/SVG/CSS: CSS for current hero; SVG for future service diagrams; canvas only for low-cost atmospheric backgrounds.
- Never animate body copy, layout-critical forms, navigation availability, or essential labels.

## H. Service presentation

### Component concept

Scope Stack: service divisions become operating layers with a signal rail, badges, explicit names, summaries, operating models, and service rhythm.

### Mobile layout

Single-column stack with a rail and compact badges. Every lane is visible without hiding key services behind icons.

### Desktop layout

Staggered command stack with a third metadata column for operating model and rhythm.

### Interaction model

Each lane links to the relevant trade/service page. Hover/tap strengthens the lane accent and nudges horizontally.

### Content model

```ts
{
  slug: string;
  badge: string;
  name: string;
  shortName: string;
  eyebrow: string;
  accent: string;
  summary: string;
  operations: {
    operatingModel: string;
    serviceRhythm: string;
  };
}
```

### Astro/Tailwind notes

Use existing `tradeProfiles` and `getTradeOperations()` for real trade lanes; add a manual managed-technology lane until the service data is normalized into the same shape.

## I. Depth and layering system

- Background: fixed grid + radial lighting + low-opacity noise.
- Midground: operating map, scope rail, signal lines.
- Content: dark translucent surfaces with hard-edged geometry.
- Foreground: CTAs, scanner tabs, badges, text links.
- Sticky elements: keep to existing nav; avoid multiple sticky CTAs.
- Texture: use SVG noise below 6% opacity.
- Lighting: top-left acid-olive, lower-right teal, both muted.
- Masks: fade grid away after the hero.
- Scroll reveals: content enters once; no looping scroll effects.
- Z-index: background -2/-1, content 1, nav/layout from existing system.
- Performance: no large video, no heavy 3D, no continuous canvas loops.

## K. Performance and quality constraints

- Mobile Lighthouse target: 95+ performance, 95+ accessibility, 95+ best practices, 95+ SEO.
- Image strategy: no above-fold remote images unless optimized and explicitly needed.
- Font loading: keep current fontsource approach, avoid adding new families.
- JS budget: homepage interaction JS under 3KB authored.
- Animation budget: transform/opacity only; no layout animating.
- CLS: reserve space for hero and panels; no injected late content.
- Tap targets: minimum 44px, preferred 52px for CTAs and scanner tabs.
- Safe area: preserve existing layout safe-area behavior.
- Reduced motion: disable ambient and signal animations.
- Keyboard: scanner tabs are buttons with ARIA states.
- Screen reader: clear heading structure and meaningful section labels.
